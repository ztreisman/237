### 500-trial library analysis

Key findings from library.jsonl:

- 173/500 trials (34.6%) reach ring 5; 0/500 reach ring 6
- Ring-5 gap: mean=11.45°, std=1.24°, range [9.67°, 16.80°]
- Ring-5 margin: mean=-2.16°, std=1.24°, range [-3.94°, +3.10°]
- 11 trials have positive global margin at ring 5 — all still collide at ring 6
- supply_deficit_frac correlates with gap (r=-0.41), stronger than edge_std (r≈0)
- Ring-6 collision edge_std: mean=0.051, std=0.001 — essentially constant

Two distinct obstruction mechanisms identified:
1. Close-in ring-5 vertices (small radius, demand → 90°): blocked by inner-ring
   triangles already occupying that R³ region
2. Far-out ring-5 vertices: ring-6 triangles crowd each other on the sphere

These are anti-correlated — reducing one increases the other. Using radius r
explicitly as a continuous variable (rather than demand_max=90° clipping) is
the right way to quantify the per-vertex obstruction.

Trial 63: gap=16.8°, margin=+3.1°, but r5_rad_std=0.910 (highest in dataset).
Large gap is artifact of extreme buckling/folding, not a near-success.
Trial 378: prof_min=15.3° (highest), deficit_frac=0.000, yet still collides.
Trial 196: lowest r6_std=0.04671 — most equilateral attempted ring-6 in dataset.

### Boundary cycle topology

Extracted ring-5 boundary cycle from PLY files:
- Confirmed: 385 vertices, all degree 2 in boundary graph, single closed cycle
- Edge lengths along cycle: mean=0.988, std=0.018 — all ~1.0, cycle correctly traced
- Radius from origin: mean=4.21, std=0.85, min=0.99, max=5.44 (ratio 5.5)
- Total curvature: ~88 × 2π (Fary-Milnor threshold is 4π for knots)
- BUT: mesh has zero non-manifold edges and no self-intersections

Conclusion: boundary cycle is topologically an UNKNOT. It must be, since it
bounds an embedded disk (the ring 0-5 surface) with no self-intersections. High
total curvature (88 × 2π) reflects geometric wildness — sharp turns at nearly
every vertex (mean turning angle 82°) — not topological complexity. Writhe
(~0.4-0.5) measures coiling of the unknot in space, not a knot invariant.

The obstruction to ring 6 is geometric, not topological.

### Proof sketch discussion

Zack has a candidate proof that n=5 is the maximum ring depth for equilateral
{3,7} embeddings in R³ with ε-thick tiles. Key ideas:

- Physical realization: tiles are ε-thick, volume constraint active
- Snapping: if vertices a,b come within ε with compatible edge directions,
  they merge into one vertex inheriting all edges from both
- A snap can trigger cascade of further snaps (new close pairs formed)
- The cascade is constrained by the Coxeter structure: each snap is a
  reflection in H₃, cascade is an orbit under the H₃ subgroup stabilizing
  the ring-5 boundary
- The algebraic mismatch between H₃ (finite, positively curved) and
  Δ(2,3,7) (infinite, hyperbolic, growth rate φ²) causes the cascade to
  reach a combinatorial contradiction at ring 6
- Both groups share φ but use it in geometrically opposite curvature contexts
- As ε → 0: volume constraint forces cascade to run to completion,
  algebraic mismatch becomes irreconcilable at ring 6 but not rings 1-5
- Rings 1-3: edge_std ≈ 0.000 (perfect); ring 4: ≈ 0.005; ring 5: ≈ 0.047
  — mismatch accumulating but not yet forcing contradiction
- The ε → 0 limit uses that snap positions are discrete (lattice points),
  so intermediate regime (not snapping, not colliding) is ruled out by
  crystal geometry

Proof requires establishing:
1. Every ring-6 candidate position is within ε of a lattice point (snap)
   OR provably non-colliding (geometric gap) — no intermediate regime
2. All snap positions at ring 6 involve degree-7 ring-5 vertices (no capacity)
3. Cascade sequences from any ring-6 snap produce H₃ relations that
   contradict required Δ(2,3,7) relations

Next steps: add snap detection to simulation to observe cascades empirically,
look at Z[φ]³ coordinates of ring-6 candidate positions.
